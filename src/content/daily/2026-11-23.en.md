The heaviest story today is not a model launch. It is a trillion-dollar company proving in ten days that it cannot function without one person. On November 22 Nvidia's board unanimously restored Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director, with a full set of constraints attached. He had resigned those three roles on November 12. During his absence the company lost more than $1 trillion in market value; within half an hour of the announcement roughly the same amount came back, and Nasdaq futures rose 7% premarket, tripping a circuit breaker.

The technology side moved too. Zhipu's GLM-5.4 put looped transformers into a shipping product by running its middle 28 layers twice. Anthropic published a VP ≠ VNP proof signed by Claude. OpenAI proposed perplexity as an industry-wide safety red line. Two separate efforts showed how to cut memory costs, and a Chinese carrier delivered its first from-scratch trillion-scale model. Evidence boundaries are marked as we go.

## 1. Reinstatement: he got the operating job back, not the old structure

On November 22 Nvidia's board voted unanimously to restore Huang as president, CEO and director, effective immediately. He had resigned the same three posts on November 12, ten days earlier. The board's release says that before deciding, it consulted senior U.S. government officials and concluded his return posed no national security risk.

During the vacancy the share price fell from $173 to $125, erasing more than $1 trillion in market value. After the announcement it rose 28% premarket, then pushed past 35% within half an hour, adding roughly $1 trillion, while Nasdaq futures climbed 7% premarket and halted. The market priced a corporate governance event as systemic risk.

The conditions matter more than the title. Independent chairman Harvey Jones stays on, and Huang gets back only the president, CEO and ordinary director seats. The independent-director investigation continues to its conclusion. The board created a National Security and Export Control Committee, also independent directors only, and the company will hire a chief compliance officer reporting to the board's nominating and governance committee.

He recovered operating control, not the old arrangement in which one person decided everything, and none of the investigations hanging over him has closed. As for the claim that the White House forced the reversal, the only support is Trump's own statement that "I convinced them" plus media relay; the board release says only that it consulted senior government officials. That causal chain remains an inference.

## 2. GLM-5.4 runs its middle 28 layers twice, and looped transformers ship

Zhipu's GLM-5.4 went live quietly. The core of the official technical note is reuse, not more parameters. The 78-layer base is cut into three segments: the first 26 layers and the last 24 run once as usual, and only layers 27–54 share one set of weights across two passes, lifting effective depth per forward pass from 78 layers to 106.

To keep the two passes from fighting, Zhipu added a per-token gate at each pass's exit: the first pass builds context, the second refines it. The second pass reuses the 2,048 sparse keys selected in the first, adds only a 128-token local sliding window per layer, and reuses the retrieval positions already computed, skipping re-indexing. This version also adds a 24-layer ViT visual projection, and the weekly quota is 50% higher than 5.3's.

In the official same-architecture 30B controlled experiment, training compute rose only about 10%, while AIME25 went from 19.17 to 30.42 and GPQA-Diamond from 47.58 to 56.26. MMLU-Pro and IFBench each gained about 3 points, and output length fell across all five benchmarks. The 750B deployment serves 200 tokens/s off-peak and 70–80 tokens/s at peak.

"750B base reaching 2–3T-class intelligence" is a vendor statement with no third-party replication, and the fixed two-pass loop is not yet adaptive compute allocated by problem difficulty. GPT-6 Astra, Inkling 2 Mini and SenseNova-Loop-9B use similar structures this quarter, the 9B running 24 of its 32 layers twice, and 2021's ladder attention is the origin of the line. Looped layers are moving from paper topic to product configuration.

## 3. A Claude-signed proof of VP ≠ VNP, on the same day the Riemann author walked his back

Anthropic published a paper on GitHub signed by Claude, claiming a proof of VP ≠ VNP, Valiant's conjecture, open for 47 years. There is no human name in the author field. The package is a 70-page paper plus more than 70,000 lines of Lean 4, and two independent checkers both pass, meaning no step from premises to conclusion was skipped.

The boundaries are stated plainly. This does not solve P vs NP; VP ≠ VNP covers only the arithmetic world of addition and multiplication. More importantly, the checkers verify the derivation, not the problem statement. The definition file is only 98 lines, and if the definitions drift, swapping the real problem for an easier variant, the checkers still go green. Anthropic itself notes that no human mathematician has reviewed it.

On the same day, the other side of this retreated. Levent Alpöge, who earlier claimed a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, responded publicly to doubts: the "250,000 pages" was a joke, the Lean code does contain a lot of junk that should have been cleaned up first, but he explicitly did not withdraw the claim. He said he had expected death threats.

Read together, credibility in AI mathematics is separating into layers: derivations a machine can verify step by step, then whether the problem statement was written correctly, then page counts and declarations alone. The 17-million-line Riemann artifact from a few days earlier cannot be checked by human eyes at all, which puts it in the third layer.

## 4. OpenAI draws a red line with perplexity, but the line does not cross companies

OpenAI's chief scientist published an internal perplexity curve and proposed using the metric to set an industry-wide safety red line; Altman reposted it as the most important piece in recent memory. The numbers on the curve: GPT-3.5 around 0.99, GPT-4 around 0.91, the just-released GPT-6.2 down to 0.76, and an unreleased internal model already at 0.73.

The accompanying account is that perplexity fell slowly for two years after GPT-4 and then dropped sharply this year. OpenAI's own footnote says the drop comes mainly from long-run research and compute investment, though AI already contributes to the design of the newest models. The chief scientist warns that AI improving training methods could bring an acceleration as early as 2027, and that once perplexity reaches 0.68–0.70 they do not know what capabilities appear, or that existing monitoring would catch them.

Safety researcher Dan Hendrycks estimates from the chart that compute efficiency for reaching the same perplexity has doubled 3 to 5 times a year since GPT-4, with much of it concentrated in the past year, possibly 100x this year. That is an outside estimate, not an official figure.

The real problem is measurement. OpenAI concedes that test material differs across labs and perplexity is not directly comparable between companies. A non-comparable metric cannot serve as an industry red line; at best it is an internal self-check. LeCun reposted a researcher who ran weekend experiments against the curve, a sign the narrative has no consensus in the field.

## 5. 8,192 Ascend 910C chips train a 285B-parameter model from scratch

China Telecom trained Xing4.1-Preview, 285 billion parameters, on 8,192 Huawei Ascend 910C chips. The technical report says it was trained from scratch with MindSpore, supports 1 million tokens, and releases the full 521GB of weights under Apache 2.0.

The key engineering choice was to shape the model around the chip rather than port an Nvidia recipe. Ascend 910C is strong on large matrix blocks but has narrow memory bandwidth, so the model is split into small experts and activates only about 17 billion parameters per pass, cutting data movement. For long context it follows DeepSeek's compression approach, storing seen content compressed and reading only what matters, which the report says speeds long-text reads by up to 4.68x.

The scorecard is restrained. Its five self-reported benchmarks compare against lightweight models of similar tier from Hunyuan and Zhipu, and most scores are slightly lower; footnotes also disclose that GPU sandbox tasks were skipped and only text was tested.

What remains is replication. The repository is not open yet, 521GB of weights will not fit on ordinary machines, and third-party testing is still blank. What is confirmed is the step itself: a domestic chip and framework training a hundred-billion-scale model end to end and releasing it fully. The capability ceiling is not yet a conclusion.

## 6. Two ways to cheapen long memory: 48 MiB of parametric memory, or KV cache on SSD

MemOS, with NVIDIA, released MemT 1.0 Pro on the Nemotron 3 Ultra base, 554B total parameters with 59B active. It stops re-injecting raw conversation history and instead compresses it in real time into a fixed persistent memory matrix read at question time: only 48 MiB of FP32 persistent memory per trajectory. Within the family, the 36B Nano needs 40 MiB and the 288B Flash 1,280 MiB.

The cost is that memory modules attach to only 12 full-attention layers. Across 11 evaluations that answer without the source text, relying on internal memory, Pro averages 53.08 for first place in its class, against 50.60 for Flash and 46.97 for Nano; the gaps come from million-scale long history, learning-while-in-use and personalized safety. But Flash still wins individual benchmarks such as MemBench, and against an external memory system or full context, 48 MiB recall remains lossy.

The other route is blunter. vLLM connected to AMD's open-source MoRI moves 1.8TB of KV cache from DRAM to enterprise NVMe, keeping only about 80GB of memory as a staging buffer. On Kimi-K3, the gap versus an equal-capacity pure-memory setup stays within 1% at 48, 70 and 96 concurrency, because 79% to 96% of reuses never leave HBM and actual disk reads hide in the queue slack of high concurrency.

At list prices, 1.8TB of DRAM costs about $76,000; enterprise SSD with five years of wear costs about $59,000 and holds a full day of cache. The authors also state the limits: the test ran on 16 MI355X cards with enterprise drives writing 83TB a day, and at low load without queue slack a disk read adds about 0.4 seconds. The interface is largely merged into vLLM mainline.

## 7. Qwen3.5 27B rewrites its own weights

Eleven researchers from UIUC, CMU, Princeton, Stanford and Yale posted a paper on November 18: with every training tool disabled and a single GPU for a few hours, Qwen3.5 27B wrote Python to modify its own weights.

It first reviewed its own practice logs and found the problem was not arithmetic, since 96% of the answers it wrote were correct, but overthinking and running past the limit mid-sentence; on one counting problem it repeated "I will write the response" 549 times until it stalled. Having found the cause, it made one change: raise by 0.5 the parameters across its 48 layers that control how fast old information decays. On 490 unseen math problems, the unfinished rate fell from 33.6% to 26.1% and accuracy rose from 57.2% to 63.8%.

The more extreme round repaired a damaged copy. Researchers replaced 8 rows of one matrix in layer 30 with random noise at 200x magnitude, and the copy scored zero on math. Told only that "some values are broken" and not shown the intact original, the model searched layer by layer, flattened the outliers, and recovered math to 33.0% against the original's 58.5%, while six visual tests went from 7.3% to 70.2% against the original's 84.3%.

Do not read this as recursive self-improvement. Each of the 8 rounds restarted from the original model, not from the previous edited version; the widely repeated "8 rounds only going up" is actually the model withdrawing 5 failed edits and committing 3 successful ones. Small models cannot do this at all: the 2B said it was a text model and could not change weights, and the 9B added noise until it fell from 47.5% to zero. The paper is an arXiv preprint validated only on the Qwen3.5 family and math problems.

## 8. The resident-agent skeleton was drawn twenty years ago

A "24-hour resident assistant" sounds new, but most of it is backend queueing. Anthropic's November 13 guide states that such agents sleep without burning quota, wake only when a new message or scheduled reminder arrives, run one round, save state and sleep again. A developer compared this against DeepSeek's open-source scheduler and found the skeletons nearly identical, both built from five parts.

The five parts: an inbox queue, where messages are written to a table and only one worker claims a job at a time so two tasks cannot overwrite each other; a timer table, where recurring jobs and follow-up reminders live and enqueue a wake-up message when due; an external notebook, holding rules, progress and items already reported, read first on waking; an approval gate, which parks high-risk actions with their context until a human nods while the agent keeps taking other inbox work; and an outbox rate limit, which official guidance suggests using so the agent stays quiet instead of popping up constantly.

That same day a paper received in November 1999 and published in 2001 in China Mechanical Engineering resurfaced. Authors Sheng Buyun and Yang Mingzhong were designing a process-planning system for factories, and the requirements they set for agents are already complete: make plans and act on its own, hold two-way dialogue with people to check intent, let differently tasked agents consult each other, and include learning and memory components so users write no code and the agent learns by observation.

What was missing then was not the skeleton but a brain that understands plain language; rules were hard-coded line by line and broke on anything unexpected. Large models supply that piece now. Conversely, the architecture diagram itself long ago stopped being a moat. What separates projects is three unglamorous jobs: cleaning up dirty business data, wiring external software's permissions and interfaces, and fixing acceptance criteria before every handoff.

## 9. On the enterprise side, the center of gravity moves from writing code to delivery and governance

Anthropic launched a resident deployment engineer curriculum: 11 courses, 9.5 hours. The first nine teach coding with Claude Code and tool integration, the basics. The two that define the job are the last two, just 100 minutes, and they are entirely about what sits outside the technology.

The delivery course covers three things. Pick work with a hard number attached: the site's example is four people over six weeks becoming two people over two weeks, with a hard result in 30 days. Record the baseline before starting, meaning how long the work took and how often it went wrong, because without it a pilot cannot say what it saved. Then watch until people actually use it: run a small team through first, then grow internal champions to teach others, surviving the early slowdown. The full resident curriculum and certification are open only to partner-company email addresses; 8 of the courses are free in the public academy.

Uber's lesson is that governance takes over once scale arrives. After growing from 1 skill to more than 5,000 in a year with over 1 million monthly calls, the focus shifts from writing a good prompt to keeping the fleet stable: three distribution tiers, Git for versions and review, and the aifx CLI to smooth over installation differences between agents. Its most valuable step is subtraction, running the same real request with and without a skill and letting a blind judge compare. In one warehouse-query experiment, runs without the skill used 13% more tool calls, hit 6x the tool errors and took 31% longer, while the skilled answer won 87% of comparisons. That is a single-skill result and cannot be generalized to platform-wide returns.

At the product level, Nadella put the shift bluntly in a podcast: code has gone from something people type to something AI emits, and people have stepped back to assigning work and accepting it. This is a relayed interview, not a Microsoft strategy document, but it points the same way as the two items above: selling "implement this spec" is getting hard to price, and the position that remains is turning vague needs into clear numbers and carrying a team through the last mile.

## Editorial conclusion

The real split today is not about model capability but about who absorbs a single point of failure. Nvidia insured one person with an independent chair, a continuing investigation and a new committee. Anthropic insured the derivation with a Lean checker and left the problem statement uninsured. OpenAI tried to draw an industry red line and stalled on a metric that cannot be compared across companies. The two engineering paths, cheaper memory and greater depth, both work now; none of the verification gaps has been closed.
